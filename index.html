聞く

An ear, set inside a gate.

The character 聞 is drawn as an ear within a gate. In kōdō, the Japanese art of incense that took its classical form some five hundred years ago, it is the word used for scent. You do not smell incense. You listen to it, letting a fragrance arrive, open and fade the way a note does.

CENKO takes its name from senkō, 線香, the Japanese word for an incense stick: a line of fragrance. We make it for that kind of attention, with unhurried sticks, quiet blends and a ritual short enough to keep every day.

儀式

A ritual in five breaths.

It takes about as long as making a cup of tea. Keep scrolling and walk through it once before you try it at home.

一

Choose a mood

Each tin holds four fragrances. Pick the one that suits the hour: cedar for a long evening, peppermint for a slow morning.

二

Hold it to a flame

Touch the tip to a match or lighter and wait until a small flame stands on its own.

三

Let the flame go out

Wave it gently with your hand until only an ember glows. Never blow on incense. Your breath is saved for listening.

四

Sit, and listen

Close your eyes. Don't chase the scent. Let it reach you in its own time, and notice how it changes as the stick burns.

五

Let the ash fall

When the stick has turned to ash, the ritual is complete. Tap the holder clean and begin again tomorrow.
